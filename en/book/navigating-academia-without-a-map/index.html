About the book

Academia has no right answers. The information often conflicts, the rules that matter go unwritten, and the results arrive long after a decision has to be made. This book is about making the next move with incomplete information, then revising that decision when more information arrives.

I first wrote this book in Korean, drawing on the problems I encountered during a sociology PhD in the United States and the first years of an assistant professorship. It offers criteria and procedures that help you see what a decision rests on while leaving the choice to you. The English edition keeps the same aim.

What you’ll find inside

The book has eight chapters on choosing a graduate program, narrowing a research question, turning a literature into an argument, finishing open-ended projects, deciding whose advice to follow, responding to rejection and review, reading unwritten rules, and building a pace you can sustain.

Each chapter starts by working out what the problem really is. It then clears away common misreadings, sets out criteria for judgment, follows those criteria through cases, and closes with a practical tool and one next action.

Nine worksheets accompany the book. They include a plan for the first 48 hours after a rejection, a meeting preparation card, a project stop checklist, and a program offer comparison table. The Excel workbook contains editable versions of all nine.

You do not need to read from beginning to end. Start with the chapter that matches the decision in front of you. The prologue offers a short reading path for applicants, graduate students at different stages, postdocs, and early-career faculty.

About the Author

Jina Lee earned a BA and an MA in sociology in Korea and a PhD in sociology in the United States. She is a sociologist working as an assistant professor, and she studies the sociology of science and gender inequality. This book grew out of a series she wrote on Threads, looking back on the years from entering graduate school through her first years on the faculty.
