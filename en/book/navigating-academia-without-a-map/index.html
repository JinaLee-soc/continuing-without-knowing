About the book

There is no right answer in academic life. The information is often contradictory, the rules that matter go unspoken, and the results come back late. What matters is how you make decisions with incomplete information and how you revise them.

I wrote this book in Korean first, for Korean students doing PhDs in the United States. I soon realized that the difficulty of learning these unwritten rules was not unique to Korean students, so I made this English edition for anyone navigating the system without an inherited map.

What you’ll find inside

Eight chapters cover choosing a graduate program, finding a research question, turning reading into an argument, finishing open-ended projects, evaluating advice, responding to rejection, reading unwritten rules, and building a sustainable pace.

Every chapter follows the same structure. It diagnoses the problem, clears away common misreadings, lays out criteria for making the decision, offers cases and a practical tool, and ends with one next action you can take today.

The PDF includes nine printable worksheets. The companion Excel workbook provides editable versions of the same tools, including a rejection response plan, meeting preparation card, project stop checklist, and program comparison table.

You do not need to read the book from beginning to end. Start with the chapter that matches the decision in front of you.

About the Author

By Jina Lee

Jina Lee did her BA and MA in sociology in Korea and her PhD in sociology in the United States. She is a sociologist working as an assistant professor, and she studies the sociology of science and gender inequality. This book grew out of a series she wrote on Threads, looking back on her path from starting graduate school through her first years on the faculty.
